# The behavioral price of integrated information

*Draft — Maxence Faldor. Companion to `phi-landscape.md` (which asks what the
definitions reward at their maxima; this paper asks what φ looks like from the
outside). Every number is generated by the executed, committed notebooks in
`experiments/` (e04, e06, e07, e08).*

## Abstract

Can integrated information be seen from the outside — from a system's behavior, its
wiring, or the effect of lesions? Using exact IIT 4.0 computations with exact
gradients (iitx), we answer by construction, at the scale where every claim can be
verified exactly. First, we make the unfolding argument quantitative at *fixed
size*: gradient descent on φ_s with behavior pinned (the visible units' k-step
transition family) removes 90% of a reference system's φ_s at 0.5% worst-case
behavioral error — but not 100%. Descent bottoms out at a **behavior-specific
floor**, and a control experiment certifies the floor is real: when the pinned
behavior is generated by a feedforward system (so a zero-φ realization exists in
the same parameter space), the same optimizer reaches φ_s = 0.0000 in 16 of 16
runs. Across eight behaviors taken from near-identical high-φ references
(φ_s ≈ 10.6), the floors span **0.000 to 2.082 ibits** — one near-maximally
integrated system's complete visible behavior requires *no* integration at all.
This defines a new quantity, the **behavioral φ-requirement**: the minimum
integration a behavior demands at fixed size, computable only by gradient descent,
splitting φ_s into a behaviorally required part and a behaviorally free surplus
that can exceed the requirement by an order of magnitude. Second, we show the
surplus is invisible to every standard probe: ten-fold φ-removal leaves the
input-dependence wiring unchanged; first-order sensitivity fails to rank lesion
effects even in graded regimes (lesions cross minimum-partition switches the
cell-local gradient cannot see); unit knockouts annihilate φ_s entirely in every
system tested, giving them zero resolving power; and the theory's own companion
quantities dissociate — big Φ *rises* while φ_s collapses under pinned descent,
doubles while the median system *loses* distinctions under free ascent, and IIT
3.0's Φ barely moves throughout. The positive conclusion is the floor itself: at
these scales, the only behaviorally meaningful integration quantity is the
requirement, not φ_s.

## 1. Introduction

The unfolding argument (Doerig et al. 2019) holds that any input–output behavior of
a high-Φ system can be reproduced by a low-Φ system, so behavioral experiments
cannot test IIT. Its published form is an existence claim that expands the network.
IIT's defenders reply that within a *fixed* substrate the claim is unestablished.
Both positions have lacked an instrument: deciding what behavior does or does not
require, at fixed resources, is an optimization problem over the space of systems —
intractable for black-box search, and Φ was long presumed non-differentiable.

iitx removes the obstacle: exact IIT 4.0 quantities, differentiable almost
everywhere with exact subgradients (validated against finite differences at 10⁻¹⁰,
and against the reference implementation PyPhi throughout — see the companion
paper). We use it to run the unfolding argument *as an experiment*, and then to ask
the broader measurement question it opens: of everything an outside scientist can
observe — behavior, connectivity, lesion effects, related measures — what, if
anything, tracks φ_s?

Method commitments (shared with the companion paper): predictions pre-registered in
each notebook before execution; only exact values called φ; every optimizer claim
paired with a control that certifies what the optimizer can find.

## 2. The unfolding experiment and the floor (e04, e08)

**Setting.** n = 4 conditionally independent binary units, two visible and two
hidden. *Behavior* is the visible-marginal k-step transition family (k = 1, 2, 3):
from each of the 16 initial states, the distribution over visible states after k
steps — a differentiable (16×3×4) tensor that pins the hidden units' functional
contribution (memory shaping future visible behavior) while leaving
behavior-invisible causal structure free. References are produced by annealed
ascent and selected for strong hidden-dependence of behavior (max total-variation
0.997 across hidden states), so the pinning is never vacuous.

**Unfolding happens — up to a floor.** Descending φ_s with a quadratic behavioral
penalty (λ swept over five decades, 16 replicas each) removes 90.4% of the
reference's 10.61 ibits at 0.5% worst-case behavioral error, and 99% at 2.3% error.
But the strictest pinned descent never reaches zero: it converges to φ_s ≈ 1.013
and stays for 900 steps, and a battery of perturbed restarts (jitter 0.05–0.5)
reconverges to within 0.002 of the same value, while *harder* pinning (λ = 10⁴)
*raises* the endpoint to 1.10 — the signature of a real constraint, not a stuck
optimizer.

**The control certifies it.** A feedforward system — hidden units evolving
autonomously, visible units reading them, no feedback — is never strongly
connected, so φ_s = 0 by definition, while its behavior still depends on hidden
state. Pinning *that* behavior, the same optimizer reaches **φ_s = 0.0000 exactly,
16/16 replicas** from random high-φ starts. When zero exists, descent finds it;
where descent stops, the behavior is responsible.

**Floors are behavior-specific and span an order of magnitude.** Across eight
references drawn from the same ascent pool (φ_s 9.62–10.61), the certified floors
are 0.000, 0.277, 0.484, 0.983, 1.014, 1.043, 1.330, 2.082 ibits. The extremes are
the finding: near-identical φ_s values conceal behavioral requirements from
*nothing* to 2 ibits. In particular one system with φ_s = 10.61 has floor 0.000 —
maximal integration, none of it behaviorally necessary.

**The quantity.** Define the **behavioral φ-requirement** R(b) of a behavior b at
fixed size as the infimum of φ_s over systems realizing b (operationally: the
certified pinned-descent floor). Then φ_s = R(b) + surplus, where the surplus —
here up to 10.6 ibits against R = 0 — is exactly what no behavioral experiment can
detect. This sharpens the unfolding debate in both directions: the strong critique
("all behavior unfolds to Φ ≈ 0") fails at fixed size (some behaviors have
R > 2 ibits), and the strong defense ("behavior constrains Φ") fails too (the
surplus is unbounded by R and dominates it in every case we measured).

## 3. The surplus is invisible to standard probes (e04, e07)

**Wiring.** The input-dependence matrix (maximal shift of each unit's conditional
under toggling each input) is ≈ 1.0 in every entry at the φ = 10.6 reference *and*
at its φ = 1.0 behavioral twin. Ten-fold φ-removal is invisible to connectivity:
integration lives in the fine geometry of the repertoires, not the wiring diagram.

**Gradients vs lesions.** The exact gradient predicts lesion effects only within a
cell of the piecewise landscape: on a graded textbook system (the OR/COPY/XOR
network, φ_s = 0.415), first-order predictions fail to rank exact single-edge
lesion effects (Spearman 0.10), because a lesion is a finite perturbation that
crosses minimum-partition switches. On a high-φ ascent endpoint the situation is
worse: every edge lesion costs 86–96% of φ_s — a flat, catastrophic map — and the
saliency map is nearly flat too (the "needle" geometry is uniformly fragile).
Partial-lesion curves distinguish the regimes cleanly (graded: 5% of φ lost at 10%
lesion; needle: 73%), but *rank* information does not survive in either.

**Knockouts.** Removing any single unit (marginalizing its inputs) annihilates φ_s
*completely in every system tested* — the loss equals the full φ_s for every unit
of both case studies. Unit-lesion studies of φ have zero resolving power: they
cannot say where integration lives because removing anything removes everything.
The theory's own mechanism decomposition does assign graded per-unit values — but
its natural validator, the knockout, is blind, leaving it unvalidated.

## 4. The theory's own companions dissociate (e04, e06)

If φ_s cannot be read from outside, can it at least be tracked by its sibling
quantities? No — in either direction of optimization:

- Under behavior-pinned **descent**, IIT 4.0's structure quantity big Φ *rises*
  from 22.15 to 24.32 while φ_s falls 10.6 → 1.0, and IIT 3.0's Φ barely moves
  (3.48 → 3.24). A system can lose ~90% of its degree of existence while its
  quantity of structure grows and the previous formalization hardly notices.
- Under free **ascent** (64 instrumented trajectories at n = 4), big Φ doubles
  (median 43.6 → 90.1) while the median trajectory *loses* a distinction
  (11 → 10 of 15): structure grows by concentrating magnitude in fewer
  distinctions, not by creating more. The process signature in Hoel's causal plane
  is determinism *with* degeneracy (the latter rising 86-fold) — ascent
  manufactures funnels, not the specialized, non-degenerate units of the classical
  IIT narrative.

## 5. Discussion

**For empirical tests of IIT.** At the scales where exact computation exists, the
program "estimate Φ from behavior, connectivity, or lesions" faces results, not
just arguments: behavior bounds only the requirement R(b), connectivity does not
change when φ_s changes ten-fold, and lesions saturate. None of this bears directly
on clinical measures (PCI and kin), which are motivated by IIT rather than
computing φ — but it does mean that at the only scales where ground truth exists,
ground truth is unreachable from the outside.

**For the unfolding debate.** Both strong positions fail quantitatively. The
refined statement: every behavior has a certified integration price R(b) ∈ [0, 2+]
ibits at this size — sometimes zero, never bounding the surplus.

**The constructive turn.** R(b) itself is well-defined, certified by a control, and
computable only with gradients (17 seconds per floor on a laptop CPU at n = 4). Its
scaling with size, horizon, and behavioral features (memory depth is the natural
predictor), and its 2026-formalism analogue, are open and now measurable.

**Limitations.** One behavior operationalization (visible k-step marginals; k = 3);
n = 4 with 2+2 visible/hidden split; conditionally independent parameterization;
floors are upper bounds on R(b) certified against a zero control but not proven
optimal; eight behaviors is a beginning, not a survey.
